- vancouverisawesome.com returns 403 on fetch. destinationvancouver.com, moa.ubc.ca, Wikipedia and vancouvertourism.org fetch fine.
- capbridge.com ticket page shows hours and offers but no dollar price; the price sits behind the booking flow. Tourism-guide sites disagree on it, so leave price to the costs agent.
- vancouvertourism.org is a guide/aggregator site, not the official board (destinationvancouver.com is). Label it press and expect its prices to differ from official ones.
- One WebFetch of a ranked-attractions page yields more discovery signal than a search. Two searches naming places plus two fetched guides gave enough for about ten discovery places.
- Search-result text alone is `snippet` access and low confidence; do not label it `read`.
- Avoid every dollar figure and the word "free" in claims and notes: the validator rejects single amounts.
- State tourism boards with a single region-wide guide page are the best one-fetch buy for a multi-place scope (michigan.org "comprehensive guide to ski resorts" covered ~35 areas). But official guides go stale: it still listed pre-rebrand resort names, and travelwisconsin.com's ski story was dated 2017. Always note the page date and cross-check names against provenance.
- islands.com fetches fine and states its method and date; useful second independent source for regional "best of" lists.
- Validator bans the stem "fundrais*" anywhere (even describing a closure) and any number followed by a currency code; it does not check em-dashes but the contract does.
- With a long provenance list and 12 calls, cover the headline places plus traps well and record the rest as a could-not-look gap rather than thin entries.
- Carried-forward prior enrichment can be cited with its original checkedOn and access; say in notes that it was not re-read.
- Seasonal/event runs: timeout.com/europe best-of pages fetch cleanly and give ranked praise plus specialities in one call. The best consensus page per call.
- lonelyplanet.com/articles/best-christmas-markets-in-europe is 404; do not guess Lonely Planet article slugs.
- europeanbestdestinations.com rankings are social-media votes, the page is over 170k chars (only the top part gets read), and venue details go stale (2026 page still named an ended Zurich market). Popularity evidence only.
- In a later wave, read the earlier agents' files for contradictions (e.g. one says an event ended, another found a successor). Recording the conflict as a finding is cheap and valuable.
- Stadium/match-day scouting (Australia, Oct 2026): searches for "pre-game pubs near X stadium" mostly return transport-authority event pages (transportnsw.info, transperth). Good for practicals and traps, useless for named pubs. City tourism boards' "pre and post game" pages (visit.brisbane.qld.au) are the one-fetch buy for precinct venues, but check the page date (Brisbane's was 2023) and confirm each named venue still trades.
- Fan-experience polls for Australian stadiums are old (Austadiums 2021 vote, AFL 2018 survey); there is no current ranking. Label the poll year.
- Distances from a venue to a framework's walked places: compute from the Wikidata venue point and the stored coordinates in src/data, call them straight-line, and say it is your arithmetic. Costs zero calls.
- Check the framework's actual place list before trusting a brief's pairing claim: the Australia brief said Sydney/Melbourne/Tasmania but australia.js has no Melbourne places.
- mona.net.au/visit fetches cleanly (hours, closed days, ferry, booking).
- German travel press (travelbook.de) and festivalsindeutschland.de come up for German market facts; the latter is an aggregator.
- Eclipse/event viewing runs: spainsolareclipse.com/best-locations-2027/ is a one-fetch multi-town table (duration, Sun altitude, short warnings). treseclipses.es viewpoint pages fetch cleanly, but they are terrain-model rankings: no access, parking or crowd data, and no page date. Use them for names and durations, never for site quality.
- space.com list articles fetch as header only; use search snippets and mark them `snippet`.
- Spanish searches for beach parking or summer vehicle rules (Bolonia, Trafalgar) return little; "Bolonia" pulls Bologna, Italy. Spend those calls on the council or local press instead, or record the gap.
- Southern West Virginia (New River Gorge, Oct 2026): visitfayettevillewv.com's nightlife page is the one-fetch buy for Fayetteville bars (dated, names ~11 venues, few hours). brilliantstream.com (WV craft-beer press) fetches cleanly via ?p= ids and dates its posts. wvtourism.com town guides fetch and show an update date. smoothambler.com is 403; greenbrierwv.com/eat-drink/ and adventuresonthegorge.com/smokeys/ were 404; several small-brewery domains did not resolve (guessing a brewery's domain wastes a call).
- "Hinton" search results mix in Hinton, Alberta (hintonrockies.com). Any small-town name shared with another place needs the state in the query and an address check.
- The word "free" hides in phrases like "toll-free", "duty-free" and "car-free" (write "without a car"); grep the output for it before finishing.
- Virginia drink scouting (Shenandoah, Oct 2026): virginiawine.org winery pages 403 on fetch, but their search snippets carry hours. monticellowinetrail.com/members fetches (member names only, no towns/hours); /wineries is 404. brewridgetrail.com 301s to nelsoncounty.com/craft-beverages (fetches, the current Nelson list). visitcharlottesville.org/wineries/breweries/ and its cideries blog fetch. visitluraypage.com drinks page redirects to a luraypagechamber.com 404; copperfox.biz/visit is 404. Guessed slugs burned 4 of 30 calls: search first, fetch only URLs seen in results.
- Small-town US drink trips: compute the trip's weekdays first; Monday/Tuesday closures (taprooms open Fri-Sun only, winery restaurants Wed-Sun) are the most common trap. Trail brands (Brew Ridge Trail) outlive their members; check each member against the current county tourism list and local press for closures and acquisitions.
- Small-town US food runs (WV, Oct 2026): local TV sites (wvva.com, woay.com, wvnstv) are the best source for closures and reopenings; Wikivoyage town pages are thin (names only). Tasting Table "best places to eat in <town>" pieces (2026) give per-venue hours, which is what exposes Sunday-closure traps. visitfayettevillewv.com/restaurants/ is a names-and-addresses directory only; its /fayetteville-food-guide/ is an empty 2021 stub. wvtourism.com (state tourism, label government) "101 unique places" list is 2017 winners re-dated 2024.
- Award lists can be published after a closure (WV Living "Best of 2025" ran Jan 27 2026 with a restaurant that announced closing Jan 9). Cross-check every award winner against local news.
- One name-specific search for an obscure venue from a founder draft is the limit; if it returns nothing, record a gap rather than retrying spellings.
- Business names containing "Our"/"We" (e.g. "Our House Grill") may trip the first-person check; paraphrase them.
- US national parks (New River Gorge, Oct 2026): nps.gov/<park>/planyourvisit/road-and-trail-closures.htm is the live closure list; conditions.htm and hours.htm can be years stale (check "Last updated"). The park's waterfalls.htm-style feature pages are the best one-fetch multi-place buy (access, warnings, vehicle limits). An NPS closures page photo caption can hint at work it does not list as a closure.
- wvstateparks.com/park/<slug>/ pages carry no facility status (tram, jetboat, lodge); its aerial-tramways page lagged a reopening by months. For WV attraction status use local TV/press (wvva.com, wvexplorer.com, herald-dispatch) and county CVBs. beckley.org/exhibition-coal-mine/ is 404.
- wvtourism.com "road trip" pages fetch cleanly and name 15+ regional sights in one call (check page date). e-WV (wvencyclopedia.org) is dated and useful as a second source but can contradict tourism pages on whether a site is open.
- Multi-part runs (eat/drink/see): WebFetch calls count toward the same budget; spend about a third on status checks for headline places, the rest on one-fetch regional lists.
- US national parks: nps.gov/<park>/planyourvisit/hours.htm carries the concession open/close table with a "last updated" date (Shenandoah's eatingdrinking.htm is 404). Concessionaire pages (goshenandoah.com/dining/<lodge>) give daily meal hours and the no-reservation rule.
- Small-town US eat scouting: searches for a county's dining return aggregator stubs (OpenTable "features", iExit, Tripadvisor). The one-fetch buys are state tourism blog guides (virginia.org/blog/post/...) and city board blogs (visitcharlottesville.org/blog/...); they name many places but rarely give closed days. Local rental-host guides (e.g. yurtofun.com) DO give days/closed days; label them aggregator.
- visitharrisonburgva.com/dining renders listings by script: fetch returns no venues. c-ville.com/best_of_c_ville_food__drink/ is the 2006 edition; do not fetch it for current winners.
- Washingtonian town food guides fetch cleanly but carry their year in the URL; flag old ones for a status check.
- Orchard/attraction ticket calendars: the URL slug year can disagree with the dates shown (chilesfamilyorchards.com cmo-entry-fall2025 showed 2026 dates). Record the conflict for the verifier.
- US national parks: don't guess nps.gov slugs (two 404s on Shenandoah). Search with allowed_domains ["nps.gov"] first, then fetch. The park's planyourvisit/hours.htm is the best one-fetch buy: dated per-facility open/close table for the season. nps.gov/blri/planyourvisit/roadclosures.htm gives dated per-milepost road status.
- nps.gov has no overlook table with mileposts or facing direction; travel-experience-live.com is the fullest list but single-site and partly inconsistent. Label mileposts as guide-sourced.
- monticello.org and omnihotels.com return 403 on fetch; use snippets. montpelier.org/visit fetches but has no hours. cartermountainorchard.com 301s to chilesfamilyorchards.com. virginia.org blog posts and luraycaverns.com/grandcaverns.com fetch cleanly.
- Day-of-week traps matter: check the trip days against each site's closed days (Monticello's own calendar showed a Tuesday closed in Aug 2026; weekend-only PYO orchards).
- Drive times from a base in 2 calls: a Wikidata SPARQL fetch for town coordinates (VALUES list of names + wdt:P131/wdt:P131? to the state QID, Virginia = Q1370; independent cities like Charlottesville drop out), then one OSRM table fetch (router.project-osrm.org/table/v1/driving/lon,lat;...?sources=0&annotations=duration,distance). Town-centre, no-traffic times only; say so.
- WebFetch on a PDF saves the binary under tool-results; Read on that file (no pages param) extracts the text when pdftoppm is missing. Greene County VA's only visitor guide (dbs-sar.com PDF) is c. 2010 and cometogreene.com no longer resolves; gcva.us refused connections; greenehistory.org fails SSL; thelafayette.com 403.
- WebSearch has a session-wide cap shared by all agents (200); in a multi-agent run it can run out mid-run. Do the searches that name places first, keep official-URL fetches for later.
- If the clock passes midnight during a run, the stop hook validates generatedOn against the local date, not RUN.json.today; checkedOn may stay on the day of the check.
- Shared session WebSearch can run out mid-run (Oct 2026 road-trip run got 7 searches, then refusals). Spend the first searches on multi-town discovery, and keep a list of official URLs to fetch directly as the fallback.
- Fetch results (road-trip run, Oct 2026): northmarket.org, schmidthaus.com, casanueva.com, jackieos.com (shows hours for "today" only, plus closure banners), trans-alleghenylunaticasylum.com/historic-tours/ (weekly schedule is an image), oglebay.com (events, no hours) and Wikipedia all fetch. zingermansdeli.com and pennalps.com 403; magwv.com refuses connection; tonypacko.com and broadporchcoffee.com render nothing useful; bigtimberbrewing.com has no hours. weelunk.com fetches but its town pieces date from 2015.
- Wikipedia coordinates for a ridge or road cut (Sideling Hill) mark the summit, not the stop: same trap as summit points; give no coordinate.
- Route-scouting with no routing budget: derive stop timing from the getting-around agent's per-leg engine hours (cumulative, times 0.78 for the low end) instead of guessing; label every detour time a scout estimate.
- Commercial trip blogs (e.g. tripprof.com) can be dated and detailed but mix in entry/border claims; use them for site character only and leave entry rules to government sources.
- Session WebSearch is shared across agents and can be refused mid-run (Oct 2026, after 2 searches). Fetch-only fallbacks that worked: dcr.virginia.gov/state-parks/<slug> (daily hours, food-service season, alerts, last-modified), fs.usda.gov/r09/monongahela/recreation/<slug> (season, days, updated date; office pages under /offices/ can be older and conflict), greenbankobservatory.org/visit/, grandcaverns.com (monthly hours), wvstateparks.com/park/<slug>/ (24/7 status only).
- Wikivoyage town pages give per-listing "updated" dates and sometimes day-by-day hours; good for Sunday checks, but listings run 2017-2026 so flag old ones. Ambiguous US towns need the state suffix (Staunton is a disambiguation page; Staunton_(Virginia) works). Lexington_(Virginia) is 404.
- Wikipedia name collisions on fetch: "Humpback_Rocks" is a South Georgia islet, not the Blue Ridge Parkway site; Falling_Spring_Falls is 404. Check the article is the right entity before citing it.
- Session WebSearch is shared across parallel agents and can be exhausted after one search (Oct 2026 road-trip run). Have a list of official URLs to fetch directly; count refused/failed attempts in callsUsed and say so in a gap.
- Road-trip runs (OH/WV, Oct 2026): fetch cleanly: tamarackwv.com, capitolmarket.net, mothmanmuseum.com, northmarket.org, jackieos.com (retry if the first fetch stalls), piesandpints.net, nps.gov/neri and nps.gov/hocu planyourvisit/hours.htm, wvstateparks.com park pages. explorehockinghills.com is the official tourism site (shuttle dates, events). Wikipedia "Midland Trail (West Virginia)" does not name the US-60 waterfalls.
- Validator checks generatedOn against the machine's date at validation time; if the run crosses midnight, set generatedOn to the new date (checkedOn may stay on the earlier day).
- Road-trip stops need an option tag and a detour cost; when nothing routes it, label detour minutes as unrouted estimates and record a detour-times gap.
- WebSearch has a SESSION-WIDE cap (200, shared by every agent in a /research run). Late-wave agents can hit it at half their own budget (Oct 14 2026: cut off after 15 of 30). Spend searches first on discovery and status checks; save WebFetch of known URLs (Wikipedia titles, NPS API, URLs already seen in results) for after the cap.
- Hinton/Summers County WV: wvtourism.com/town-guide-hinton/ is 404. waywardblog.com/hinton-summers-county-west-virginia-travel-guide is dated (updated Aug 2026) and names addresses. - History runs (Oct 2026): WebSearch with allowed_domains ["wvencyclopedia.org"] returns several e-WV entries per call with rich snippets; one search can name 3-4 topics. - History claims: avoid dollar figures (costs, settlements) since the validator treats them as point prices; record contested numbers (death tolls, heights, dates) with every estimate and its source in notes.
- History runs (WV, Oct 2026): e-WV fetches cleanly at wvencyclopedia.org/entries/<id> and /articles/<id> (author + "last revised" date); search with allowed_domains ["wvencyclopedia.org"] to get the id, never guess it. nps.gov/<park>/learn/historyculture/<slug>.htm pages fetch with "last updated" and are the best one-fetch buy per site; snippets of the same search often carry enough for a second NPS page. NPS "news" pages (e.g. neri falconcam.htm) can be archived single-year releases: skip them for program history.
- Legends need their contested status recorded: NPS itself labels some local stories folklore (Dunglen poker game); state encyclopedias may omit rival claims (e-WV John Henry entry skips the Lewis Tunnel theory), so search the rival claim separately.
- The validator's money regex treats "<number> pounds" as currency: convert weights to tons or kg in claims and notes.
- History runs (Shenandoah, Oct 2026): nps.gov/articles/<slug>.htm and nps.gov/shen/learn/historyculture/*.htm fetch cleanly and are dated; NPS pages often disagree with each other on counts (CCC camps 10 vs 12, displaced families 465 vs 500+), so record the conflict rather than pick. nps.gov/places/* pages are thin (no dates). hmdb.org returns 403 on fetch (snippets only). va250.org/?p=<id> serves Virginia marker text and fetches. usgs.gov geology-and-ecology-of-national-parks pages fetch and beat NPS nature pages for geology.
